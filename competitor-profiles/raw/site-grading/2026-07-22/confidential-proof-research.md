# Confidential-client proof research

Date: 2026-07-22

## Sources

- FTC, Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule Q&A: testimonials displayed by a business are advertising; fake or false testimonials can create liability. https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers
- Equinet Media, anonymous B2B case studies: obtain approval, agree on anonymity, replace brand authority with contextual specificity, foreground measurable outcomes, use real quotes, and explicitly state that the client requested anonymity. https://www.equinetmedia.com/blog/how-to-write-a-case-study-anonymous-client
- SalesHive homepage: category, result, operating mechanism, human/platform split, proof, process, risk reduction, and CTA are all explicit. https://saleshive.com/

## Evidence-backed implications for Scorecard

- Do not replace a client name with a vague phrase such as “a leading B2B company.” Use a bounded description: industry, scale, sales motion, growth stage, and relevant operating constraint.
- Mark each story “Client name withheld by agreement” so anonymity reads as professional discretion rather than missing proof.
- Obtain written permission for the anonymized wording and final published version.
- Use exact, supportable before-and-after measures and timeframes. Do not use illustrative metrics as implied client outcomes.
- Attribute real quotes by seniority and company context when the client approves, for example: “CRO, $30M industrial distributor.”
- Show sanitized deliverables and decision artifacts. Clearly label reconstructed or demonstration data rather than presenting it as a live client screenshot.
- Explain Scorecard's contribution without implying sole causation when other people, channels, or market conditions affected the result.
- Build credibility through method proof, artifact proof, founder/operator credentials, and a lower-risk initial engagement while public customer proof is limited.
