# Competitive website baseline

Date: 2026-07-22

Grades assess what the website makes a buyer believe, not the quality of the company behind it.

## Grade key

- **A+**: Category-defining. Clear, credible, differentiated, proven, and easy to buy.
- **A**: Strong enough to create conviction; one material gap keeps it from category-defining.
- **B**: Credible and competitive, but requires the buyer to infer or take something on faith.
- **C**: Understandable, but generic, under-proven, or confusing.
- **D**: Actively reduces confidence.

## Baseline conclusion

Scorecard's **strategy and visual presentation are already competitive**. Its homepage is more coherent than many B-grade competitors and more ambitious than most tool-only sites.

Its limiting factor is **evidence density**. The current page contains almost none of the trust infrastructure used by A-level competitors: named customers, named operators, quantified case studies, third-party validation, genuine product views, and a clear explanation of what the software does versus what the experts do.

The shortest path from **B to A** is not another positioning rewrite. It is to make the current promise believable:

1. Add one flagship, end-to-end case showing target → plan → ranked accounts → action → result → learning.
2. Show the actual product at the moment it changes a decision.
3. Introduce the experts and their judgment, not only the services.
4. State the division of labor: what Scorecard's system does, what Scorecard's people do, and what the client does.
5. Replace illustrative metrics with clearly labeled real evidence—or remove them until real evidence exists.
