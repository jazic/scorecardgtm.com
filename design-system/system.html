Scorecard · Design System

The signal in the noise — encoded.

A reference for everyone shipping Scorecard surfaces. Logo, color, type, components, and motifs — all built around a single visual idea: most prospects are noise, a few are the signal.

01 · Logo

The mark

Three overlapping curves form a distribution silhouette — two muted bumps for the noise, one tall colored bump for the signal. The wordmark sits at the same baseline as the curves' base.

06 · Voice

Tone principles

Four rules of thumb for copy and visual decisions.

Plain over poetic
Say "find more of your ideal customers" — not "unlock latent revenue." Read every line out loud; if it sounds like ad copy, rewrite.
Numbers earn their place
A score, a count, or a calibration figure should always be specific and honest. "8,481 scored, 127 cleared" beats "thousands of qualified leads."
Show the model, don't market it
When in doubt, expose the rubric. The product is more credible when the user can see what the score is made of.
Quiet by default
Soft neutrals carry most surfaces. Brand color appears where it adds meaning — the signal peak, a tier badge, a status pulse — not as decoration.
