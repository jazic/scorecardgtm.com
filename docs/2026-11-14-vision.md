**Updated:** 14 November 2026. Supersedes `docs/2026-07-16-website-brief.md` (July), which described a managed service. Scorecard is not one.

This is the marketing-side source of truth: what the site says, to whom, and how it looks. Product truth lives in the app repo,

## What Scorecard is

**Self-serve SaaS for finding the accounts worth a seller's effort.** You describe the businesses you want in plain words.
Scorecard finds US companies that match, checks the evidence and ranks them by what matters to you.

Headline: **Target prospects worth the extra effort.**

Three agents do the work, and the site shows them working:

1. **Search** sizes the market (every US business → your type → your trait → your territory).
2. **Research** checks the facts against websites and public records, answers your Signals and finds the owner.
3. **Score** weights your priorities, grades every company A–D and puts the best fits first.

**Signals** are the questions Scorecard answers for every company: built-in ones, plus custom ones you write.
Each answer comes with its source.

## What it isn't

- Not a lead database. The point is fewer, better accounts, not a bigger export.
- Not a cold-email or volume tool. Scorecard finds who deserves a personal touch; it doesn't send the touch.
- Not a black-box score. Every finding shows its source.
- Not a managed service or consultancy. The customer runs it. "Challenge Scorecard" and Teams pilots are on-ramps, not the product.

## Who it's for

Lean teams without a RevOps or research function. All of them share one job: stop guessing which accounts deserve attention.

## Voice

From the app's brand values (`docs/app/04 design.md`) and how the live copy already reads:

- **Specific.** Concrete businesses and traits ("landscapers in California that plow snow"), never "leverage AI to unlock pipeline."
- **Honest about gaps.** Unknown stays unknown. Say what isn't in any record (revenue, willingness to sell) rather than implying it.
- **Confidently picky.** Celebrate the few accounts worth effort, not the size of the list.
- **Skeptical of hype.** Show the agents working and the sources behind findings; don't promise magic.
- **Humans buy from humans.** Scorecard clears the research so sellers can spend effort on people (`docs/app/00 mission.md`).

Use: *accounts, Signals, sources, score, grade, territory, owner, the person to approach.*
Avoid: *managed service, operators, growth system, leads at scale, AI-powered.*

## Launch and marketing execution

Working direction, not evidence of product–market fit.

## The ambition

Build a self-serve prospecting product that helps lean sales teams, founders, and acquisition searchers find the companies they mean, check the evidence, and decide which accounts deserve their time. Customers should get useful research without assembling a complicated workflow or sorting through a noisy database.

Launch with a small budget and a repeatable learning process. Use free organic distribution to discover who gets recurring value, which problems matter enough to pay for, and what prevents people from succeeding. Earn the first ten paying customers, make the same motion repeatable to thirty, then work toward one hundred active paying accounts. These are checkpoints, not proof of product–market fit or a promised timeline.

## Scrappy execution, systematic learning

Start with one narrow customer segment and one real prospecting task. The initial hypothesis is small B2B teams that repeatedly sell into a fragmented industry and territory. Sales teams, founders, and acquisition searchers remain potential buyers, but each experiment should have a clear audience. Change the segment when customer evidence warrants it.

Every experiment needs an audience, a problem, a message, a next action, and a result we can learn from. Keep a simple record of what we tried, who responded, whether they found useful accounts, and what happened afterward. Repeat what brings qualified users back to the product. Stop activity that creates content or signups without useful research, repeat use, or payment.

Organic distribution avoids media spend; it still costs time and research credits. Track both so the launch does not become an unbounded manual research service.

## Distribution built from real product use

- **Posting and community participation.** Turn real searches into useful posts: the query, a match, the evidence, a rejected near-match, and what we learned. Start with X and one relevant community such as Searchfunder or a permitted Reddit discussion. Participate and answer questions rather than only dropping links. Justin can speak as himself in these channels. LinkedIn stays Scorecard-branded or uses a real, willing representative; Justin's personal LinkedIn profile is not the primary launch channel.
- **Search and SEO.** Publish pages that answer specific customer questions about finding companies, qualifying accounts, and researching a market. Begin with questions heard in demos and communities. Use real examples and a clear path to a trial. Turn a useful, proven format into a repeatable publishing template only when each page has distinct value. Avoid bulk pages that merely swap industry or city names. SEO builds over time and should run alongside direct conversations, not delay them.
- **The prospect-search challenge.** Use a dedicated campaign page and relevant posts to invite queries. The working offer is up to 100 candidates with the strongest ten highlighted and supporting evidence, fewer when warranted. Deliver the promised result, ask what is useful or wrong, then invite the person to repeat or refine the task in a trial. Confirm capacity, turnaround, and fulfillment before promoting it.
- **Use Scorecard to find its own customers.** Build small, reviewed batches of companies that need to prospect a fragmented market. Bring a relevant example to the conversation and learn whether the problem recurs. The buyers of Scorecard are often the businesses selling into a demo market, not the companies appearing in the demo results.
- **Email and audiences.** Offer an optional subscription for useful examples and updates. Follow up with trial users about their task and progress. Separate list delivery and product messages from marketing opt-ins. Set up appropriate consent and measurement for future advertising audiences, but do not mistake audience size for demand.

The homepage sells the product with **Start free trial** as its primary action. The challenge is a small secondary link to its own page, not the central product offer. CRM integration and MCP access remain coming soon until verified. Unverified counts, capabilities, pricing, and trial terms stay flagged in working materials.

## Agents make the work repeatable; people build understanding

Use agents to research public conversations, organize feedback, prepare account research, draft posts and SEO pages, repurpose approved demos, and summarize experiment results. Reuse briefs, templates, and a small content backlog so each week's work builds on the last.

Justin owns demos, substantive customer conversations, judgment about feedback, and the relationship with early users. Agents can prepare a demo and summarize notes, but a human should watch where users struggle and ask why. Human contact is part of product discovery, not something to automate away as soon as possible.

Public content and outreach receive human review before publication or sending. Agents do not invent customer proof, claim unavailable features, impersonate users, or autonomously buy ads. Marketing agents helping us operate are separate from customers receiving MCP access to Scorecard.

## A weekly operating rhythm

Use this as a starting cadence and adjust to actual capacity:

1. Choose one audience/problem hypothesis and one conversion question for the week.
2. Run three to five conversations, demos, or observed trials with relevant users. Use their real task. Friends can test usability, but their encouragement is not demand validation.
3. Turn one useful session into two posts and one substantive search-oriented page or improvement to an existing page. Reuse evidence, not identical promotional copy everywhere.
4. Review the largest repeated barrier to a useful shortlist. Fix it, then ask an affected user to try again. Log requests separately from observed problems.
5. Review results by source and cohort. Decide what to repeat, change, or stop next week.

The loop is continuous: customer task → product use → feedback → product improvement → useful public example → next qualified user. Marketing should make the product better, and product improvements should supply the next credible marketing story.

## What counts as progress

Track the path from a relevant conversation or visit to a trial, a useful shortlist, a second real task, payment, and renewal. Record the user's segment, acquisition source, help required, and research cost. Ask which accounts they would actually pursue and which results were wrong.

A useful shortlist is one the customer judges actionable for their stated task. Activation is not merely signing up or running a query. Repeat use should reflect another genuine task, not a request from us to click around. Likes, impressions, email subscribers, and testimonials are supporting signals; they do not substitute for return use and willingness to pay.

For the first ten qualified trials, the existing working targets are five useful shortlists and three purchases after the normal trial. These are diagnostic targets for a small learning batch, not statistical benchmarks. If users cannot get useful results, improve the product or targeting. If results are useful but people do not pay or return, investigate recurring need, urgency, and the offer before increasing distribution.

Paid ads come later as a capped experiment when we have a credible message and understand activation, retention, research costs, and support effort. The immediate priority is organic learning and helping real users succeed.
