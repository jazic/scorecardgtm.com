## Point of view: reach 100 paying customers by proving one repeatable use case

### Pushback on the original plan

- **An email list and a pixel audience are assets, not evidence of demand.** Set up lightweight capture, then concentrate on customers reaching value and paying. Do not spend the first month collecting people who only want free reports.
- **Organic does not mean passive publishing.** A new brand needs relevant distribution: useful participation where buyers already discuss their problem, colleague introductions and small targeted account batches. Posting to an empty company page alone is not the plan.
- **Friends and family should not become a launch phase.** Run a handful of usability sessions alongside real buyer recruitment. Compliments and testimonial promises are weaker evidence than repeat use and payment.
- **Use Scorecard for acquisition earlier.** A small researched account batch belongs in the first-customer experiment, not weeks after a content program is established. It produces conversations and product evidence at the same time.
- **A broad audience makes learning slow.** Sales teams, founders and acquisition searchers have different jobs. Keep the product open, but select one campaign segment and one recurring task for the first experiment.
- **A cheap subscription cannot support unlimited concierge work.** Hands-on demos are research for the first customers. Record how much help each person needs and reduce that help before scaling.
- **Ads cannot fix a product people use once and abandon.** Test retention and research costs before increasing acquisition volume. Paid distribution is optional; it is not a required graduation ceremony.

## Competitive positioning

Justin's strategic shorthand: **a cheaper Clay, a smarter ZoomInfo, and a more AI-friendly tool.** This is the intended competitive direction, not a verified comparative performance claim.

**Customer-facing promise:** Find the companies you mean, without building a research workflow or sorting through a noisy database.

| Direction | Customer benefit | Evidence needed before comparative advertising |
|---|---|---|
| Cheaper Clay | Useful research with less workflow setup and lower total cost | Compare the same task, output quality, research/enrichment usage, setup effort and ongoing cost; subscription prices alone are insufficient. |
| Smarter ZoomInfo | Match what a business actually does, show fit evidence and reduce irrelevant results | Test the same queries against an agreed rubric; compare useful coverage and false positives. Do not imply universal data or contact superiority. |
| More AI-friendly | Describe a search naturally and, eventually, use Scorecard from the AI tools where work happens | Distinguish the natural-language product experience from agent access. API/MCP search, research, structured results and source links are a product direction until implemented and verified. |

Lead with outcomes in public copy. Competitor names can frame a customer's existing frustration without asserting untested savings or superiority. “AI inside the product” does not by itself mean an external AI agent can use it. Keep unsupported integrations out of launch promises.

## Challenge offer and subscriber conversion

**Hook:** What companies are you struggling to find at scale?

**Offer:** Give us a query, geography and fit criteria. For the first ten accepted searches, get up to **100 candidate companies**, with the **10 strongest matches highlighted and evidence behind their fit**. Return fewer if the evidence does not support those counts; never fill the list with noise or call the top ten “A-grade” by default. Rehearse quality, cost and turnaround before publishing the promise.

The free deliverable includes the promised list and top-match evidence without a surprise signup gate. Candidate discovery is distinct from confirmed qualification: show research status, unknowns and the rubric, with the deepest manual review on the top matches.

The subscription buys repeatable access to the research capability, not gratitude for a free list:

1. Capture the real task, criteria, timing and whether the need recurs. Email for delivery is separate from an optional marketing subscription.
2. Deliver the list and ask which three they would contact and which are wrong.
3. Offer **Continue this search in Scorecard**. Preserve query, criteria and results using supported functionality; otherwise provide the exact query and a short walkthrough. No assumed saved-search/import capability.
4. Have the user refine criteria and generate their own useful shortlist during the trial.
5. Prompt a second real task: another territory, next account batch, another client or a new thesis.
6. Offer the normal subscription with verified pricing and actual trial expiry. Measure payment, repeat use and renewal separately from email opt-ins.

Do not start trials or create accounts before users choose signup. Do not make recurring manual research the paid offer. API/MCP access is not an advertised upgrade until available. Detailed drafts and fulfillment: `docs/plans/2026-11-13-prospect-search-challenge.md`.

## Track 1 — Organic discovery, email, audiences and fast learning

**Job:** Attract people with the problem, help them experience value, and learn where they get stuck.

Use Scorecard-branded, screen-led demonstrations rather than Justin's personal LinkedIn profile. LinkedIn is a supporting brand surface initially, not the primary acquisition bet. Justin is comfortable participating personally on X, Reddit and Searchfunder; choose one based on where the first segment has relevant conversations and respect each community's participation rules. Use a real representative or customer only with their participation and approval. Keep the website and email list as durable destinations. Brand-led does not require hiding who operates the business in customer conversations.

Start with two useful demonstrations per week, repurposed from the same real work. Choose from these formats rather than funding three separate content streams:

- **Show a search:** A real, narrow business description, the territory, a few matches, and why they fit. Link to the relevant example and trial.
- **Show the evidence:** Open one company's signal and source. Explain an unknown or false positive and what changed after checking it.
- **Show what we learned:** A user struggled, a criterion was ambiguous, or an assumption failed. Show the product correction and invite the next test.

Batch source material from actual product sessions and a short private conversation with Justin; the public asset can be a branded screen recording without his face. AI drafts from that material. Justin reviews for accuracy and voice. No invented founder stories or claims that a fictional example generated revenue.

Build one reusable sample market report with a clear trial CTA. Offer an optional email subscription for additional market examples and product updates; explain what subscribers receive. Keep trial/service messages distinct from marketing subscriptions. Do not force a newsletter signup just to inspect the product proof.

**Audience instrumentation:** Choose ad platforms when the campaign audience is clear. Prepare the site's consent controls and corresponding pixel tags before public promotion. Where permission is required, tags must wait for it. A website audience, an email list and a trial cohort are different assets; do not treat one as automatic permission to use the others for advertising. Track only useful website actions, not uploaded customer lists, search criteria or researched contact data. Pixel audiences may remain too small for delivery early on.

**Feedback loop:** Invite trial users to share the business description they were trying to search, where they stopped, and one result that seemed wrong. Log the issue, affected users, evidence, severity and owner. Prioritize broken core actions first, then repeated barriers to a useful shortlist. After a fix, ask an affected user to repeat the task. Publish a short weekly update tied to the problem solved.

## Track 2 — Friendly testing and credible proof

**Job:** Find friction cheaply and get evidence from users who actually have the problem.

Friends and family can test comprehension, signup and navigation even when they are not buyers. Their feedback does not validate market demand. Give them a concrete task: describe a business type, find a match, identify why it fits and find the person to approach. Observe where help becomes necessary.

Colleagues who prospect or source acquisitions should use their own real market and criteria. Ask what they would have done without Scorecard, which result they would act on, which result was wrong, and whether they return to run another search. Tag these users as a recruited cohort so their activity does not get mistaken for independent organic acquisition.

Ask for testimonials after use. Preserve the person's wording and obtain approval for the quote, attribution and intended use. Prefer a concrete account of the task completed over general praise. Do not write the outcome for them or imply a paid customer relationship that does not exist. A friendly relationship should not be disguised as independent customer evidence.

Initial planning target: 5–10 observed sessions, including at least 3 people with a real prospecting/search job. This is a manageable learning batch, not a statistical validation threshold.

## Track 3 — Use Scorecard to find Scorecard's customers

**Job:** Make the product itself the source of a qualified target list and credible demonstrations.

Build the first 25-account batch during the first-customer experiment, alongside organic demonstrations and colleague testing. Expand toward 50 only after reviewing results. The account we market to is the company that needs to find SMBs, not automatically the SMB in our demo. For example, a company selling equipment or services to commercial landscapers may buy Scorecard; a landscaper found in that search is usually its prospect.

Define the buyer criteria first: sells to many businesses in a fragmented market, benefits from geography or operational details that generic industry categories miss, and has someone responsible for prospecting. Research only what the evidence supports. Record fit, source, source date, unknowns and an appropriate person to approach. Use the product to perform this work and record manual corrections honestly.

Justin reviews the list. Start relevant conversations with a useful example of that company's market and a specific invitation to try it. Do not claim public engagement is purchasing intent. AI may research and draft, but sending is a separate human decision.

Track account fit → conversation → trial → useful shortlist → return usage → paid. Capture objections and incorrect matches as inputs to both the product and marketing. A dogfooding case study can show the actual workflow immediately; it can claim acquisition results only after those results happen.

## Track 4 — Early paid learning; scale after proof

**Job:** Use a small, capped experiment to recruit qualified testers during discovery; amplify only after the message and product journey work with relevant people. See the follow-up research below for early-test ideas.

Start with the most convincing real demonstration and approved colleague proof. Choose one paid channel based on where qualified users actually came from and the available audience. Test a small number of genuinely different angles: finding a narrow market, verifying fit, and identifying who to approach. Do not produce dozens of cosmetic variants before knowing which problem resonates.

Run retargeting only if the eligible audience can deliver; otherwise it is not a viable first campaign merely because a pixel exists. A separate, tightly scoped acquisition campaign can test the strongest organic angle once the budget and conversion path are ready.

No spend is set in this report. Before launch, choose a total test cap, acceptable cost per activated trial and paid-customer economics using actual research costs, gross margin and retention assumptions. $59/month is revenue, not an available acquisition budget. Judge creative by qualified activation and downstream conversion, not the cheapest signup.

Do not build an autonomous ad buyer in phase one. AI can assemble briefs, draft variants and summarize results. Justin approves creative, audience, budget and changes to spend.

## Measurement and progression

Define an **activated trial** as a user completing a search in their own market, inspecting at least one sourced answer, and saving or exporting a shortlist they identify as useful. This is the proposed success definition; verify that the app can instrument these actions. Signup alone is not activation.

Maintain a small weekly table by cohort: organic, friends/family, colleagues, Scorecard-sourced outreach, and eventually ads. Record visitors where measurable, email opt-ins, trials, activated trials, return use, paid conversions and the main reason users stopped. Use UTMs and self-reported discovery together; do not claim perfect attribution.

Expand ad spending when the intended signup-to-shortlist path works without rescue, relevant users repeatedly reach value, there is at least one honest demonstration, and measurement can distinguish signups from activation. These are readiness criteria, not promises of commercial success. If activated users do not return, investigate product value before buying more traffic.

### Weekly scoreboard and immediate priorities

Review qualified trials, activated trials, return use, new paid accounts, cancellations, active paid accounts, first-renewal rate for eligible cohorts, support minutes per activated account and research cost per paid account. Break these out by acquisition source and friendly versus independent users. Record cohort sizes; early percentages are unstable.

**First week:** Pick the segment and talk to five buyers; verify the signup-to-shortlist journey; build one real market example and the first 25-account list; invite the first 10 qualified testers; run a few friend/family usability sessions in parallel. Publish two useful examples in one relevant channel. Email capture and permitted pixel setup should support this work, not hold it hostage.

**Then:** Review the first cohort's activation, fix the largest repeated blocker, and follow the trial through to payment. Do not call a two-week trial commercially validated after three days. The next recruitment batch should test whether the fix helped.

### Planning the distance from 30 to 100

There is no observed conversion baseline yet. Use scenarios to expose the workload, not forecast success. To add 70 paid accounts before allowing for churn:

| Qualified trial → paid assumption | Qualified trials needed |
|---|---:|
| 10% | 700 |
| 20% | 350 |
| 30% | 234 |

At an illustrative 20% rate, 50 qualified trials a month produce about 10 new paid accounts a month before churn; the final 70 would take seven months without churn. That makes the real question concrete: can the chosen channel produce enough qualified trials while existing customers stay? Replace these assumptions with observed cohorts. Do not set a 100-customer deadline until there is evidence for the required acquisition rate and retention.

For ads, define an allowable customer-acquisition cost from measured gross contribution and a chosen payback period. Then multiply that cap by observed trial-to-paid conversion to estimate an allowable cost per trial. Include paid research and enrichment costs in contribution. No budget or payback target is approved by this report.
