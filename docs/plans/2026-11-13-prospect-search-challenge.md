# The Scorecard prospect-search challenge

Working campaign draft · November 13, 2026 · No posts, messages or ads have been sent.

## Campaign decision

**Public hook:** What companies are you struggling to find at scale?

**Positioning:** Find the companies you mean, without building a research workflow or sorting through a noisy database. Justin's internal shorthand is a cheaper Clay, a smarter ZoomInfo and a more AI-friendly tool. Comparative cost/quality claims require task-level proof. Natural-language search and future external-agent access are separate; API/MCP availability is not assumed.

**Offer:** Give us a query, US geography and what makes a company a great fit. For the first ten accepted searches, we will use Scorecard to deliver up to **100 candidate companies**, with the **10 strongest matches highlighted and evidence behind their fit**. If evidence supports fewer candidates or fewer strong matches, return fewer and explain the gap. Do not pad the list or force ten A grades. The list is free in exchange for honest feedback; independent continuation uses the normal trial and subscription.

### What is delivered

- A downloadable list of up to 100 candidate companies: name, website, geography, relevant observed signals, research status, fit rationale and sources where available. Unknown fields remain unknown; candidates are not all represented as verified matches.
- A highlighted top set of up to ten, manually reviewed against the agreed criteria with criterion-level evidence, dates and remaining uncertainties. Identify the person to approach only where supported; do not promise verified phone/email coverage.
- The exact query, criteria and weights/rubric used, so the requester can judge and repeat the search.
- A relevant exclusion example when available, showing why a superficially similar company did not qualify.

Deliver the promised list without a surprise signup, newsletter subscription or payment gate. Public demonstrations need permission and should omit private criteria or contact details.

Seek real tasks people need to complete soon, using publicly checkable criteria. Examples include commercial rather than residential customers, an advertised service, geography or publicly identified ownership. Private financials and willingness to sell are not promises. Avoid selecting only the “hardest” searches: we need feasible recurring tasks, not impossible requests.

The experiment tests whether useful research leads to independent product use and payment. Ten accepted requests is a proposed real capacity limit, not manufactured scarcity. Rehearse three representative queries before publishing: count usable candidates and strong matches, record research/enrichment costs, manual minutes and gaps, and verify that the trial can reproduce the core value. Set turnaround from those results; do not promise a fixed delivery time before measuring it.

**Primary action:** Send my search.

Show a completed example before the form. Explain that this is a one-time product challenge with manual review of the top matches, not a recurring managed service. State the normal trial and $59/month continuation offer once product/billing terms are confirmed. Show the real batch capacity and confirmed turnaround.

Ask only for:

1. Business type and geography.
2. Two must-have criteria.
3. What the requester will do with the results, when they need them, and whether this is a one-time or recurring task.
4. Email for list delivery. Allow public commenters to receive a suitable public result/list link in-thread instead; do not impose a surprise email gate after offering a public answer.

Marketing updates are a separate optional checkbox, unchecked by default. Public reuse of their search or feedback requires separate permission. Do not request a CRM upload, confidential financial information or private contact lists for this experiment.

**Qualification:** Accept tasks within supported US coverage and publicly observable evidence. Aim for real near-term tasks, not hypothetical curiosity. If a search is unsuitable, explain why and do not start a trial on the person's behalf. Collect genuine demand outside current scope as research; do not overpromise to win the signup.

## Turning the list into subscribers

| Step | What the person gets | Next action | What we learn |
|---|---|---|---|
| Request accepted | A restatement of criteria and delivery expectation | Confirm the task if ambiguous | Is there a real job and a clear success condition? |
| List delivered | Up to 100 candidates and the strongest 10 with reviewed evidence, plus unknowns | Name three they would contact and flag wrong fits | Does the output help? |
| Start their own trial | **Continue this search in Scorecard** with query, criteria and results preserved where supported | Refine the criteria themselves and run the search | Can they reach value without us doing all the work? |
| First useful shortlist | Source inspection, prioritization and existing save/export workflow | Use the result for the actual job | Is this activation rather than browsing? |
| Second task | A new geography, next batch or another real criterion | Come back and repeat the workflow | Is there a recurring need? |
| Paid decision | Clear current plan, entitlements and actual trial expiry | Subscribe if continuing is worth the price | Does usefulness translate into payment? |

The conversion handoff must preserve context. If saved-search links or account imports already work, use them after signup with permission. Otherwise provide the exact query, criteria and a short walkthrough. Do not make building a new integration a prerequisite. Do not create accounts or start trial clocks before users choose to sign up. Verify that the trial contains enough capabilities to reproduce the promised core value; do not give an apparently free experience that only works with hidden premium reads.

### Two different subscriber outcomes

**Email subscriber:** Explicitly opts into useful examples and product updates. This is a nurturing audience, not a paying customer. Do not automatically subscribe everyone who requests a list.

**Paid product subscriber:** Uses Scorecard independently for a second useful task and chooses a subscription. Optimize for repeat research needs: another territory, next batch, another agency client or another acquisition thesis. Do not manufacture extra work just to consume credits.

The subscription buys ongoing access to the capability. A recipient who only wants one list may be satisfied by the free delivery; record that rather than forcing a false recurring-use story. One repair to a misunderstood query can protect trust, but ongoing manual revisions should not become the hidden product.

### Follow-up logic

- If the sample was not useful: ask why and correct one clear misunderstanding if feasible. Do not push an upgrade.
- If the sample was useful but they did not start: ask what blocks continuing. Send a single relevant follow-up rather than an extended generic sequence.
- If they started but stalled: identify the actual point of failure; offer a brief private demo or one concrete instruction.
- If they activated: ask about the next real search. Encourage repeat value instead of consuming credits for its own sake.
- Before actual trial expiry: state the true expiry date and supported paid benefits. Use their verified activity only; never invent personalized usage claims.
- If they paid: check whether the first paid research task worked. Request testimonial permission after a concrete result, not as the price of receiving the free sample.

Emails here are drafts. Sending, automation and campaign deployment are separate actions.

## Milestones and stop rules

For the first ten accepted searches, proposed diagnostic goals are: five people identify a useful result, four start a trial, three complete their own useful shortlist, and one or two pay after the full trial window. These are new campaign-funnel hypotheses, not benchmarks or a replacement for the main report's qualified-trial targets: the denominators differ.

Record channel, task/segment, acceptance reason, sample usefulness, trial, activation, second task, payment, renewal, research cost and human minutes. Track friendly testers separately. Public engagement and email opt-ins are secondary measures.

- Few qualified requests: review distribution, message and form friction; do not infer product failure from no traffic.
- Many requests, poor samples: stop buying more demand and fix targeting or research quality.
- Useful samples, no self-serve activation: the sample-to-product handoff or onboarding is failing, or the audience wants a service.
- Activation, no payment: investigate recurring need, paid value and offer clarity.
- Payments, no repeat usage or renewal: improve retention before expansion.

Use the three-query rehearsal to set an affordable fulfillment timebox and cost limit for 100 candidates plus top-ten review. Do not carry forward the earlier three-match sample time estimate. Record automated research/enrichment cost and manual verification separately. Pause new acceptance if the offer cannot be fulfilled honestly within capacity; do not silently downgrade an accepted promise or pad results. The challenge must not turn Scorecard back into a managed service.
