The desktop has a coherent visual identity and a useful demonstration. The biggest gaps are **mobile readability, overstated coverage, illustrative “proof,” and unexplained credits**. Keep the dark agent bars; they give the walkthrough continuity.

Scores below reflect the current screenshots and source. **C = copy; V = visual/UX.** Mobile scores apply only where screenshots were supplied; other mobile recommendations remain unverified.

**1. Navigation — C 8/10 · V 8 desktop, 7 mobile**

Clear primary action, but mobile loses Sign in and section navigation. Several destinations are placeholders.

**Fix:** Keep “Start free trial.” Add a compact mobile menu containing “How it works,” “Pricing,” and “Sign in.” Connect actual destinations before launch. Keep the logo animation subordinate to navigation.

**2. Hero — C 7/10 · V 8 desktop, 7 mobile**

The headline fits the positioning. The paragraph overpromises “every matching business” and guaranteed owner identification. The map dominates the actual deliverable; mobile removes that deliverable entirely.

**Replace paragraph:**
“Describe the businesses you sell to. Scorecard finds matching US companies, checks the evidence and ranks them by fit—with owner names where available. You choose who to approach.”

**Layout:** Keep the headline. Enlarge the result card and reduce the map’s prominence. Label it “Illustrative example.” On mobile, show a compact result card beneath the CTA instead of hiding all product imagery. Replace the red scribble with a precise locator and connector.

**3. Who it’s for — C 8/10 · V 8/10**

Good audience distinction. Tiny diagrams introduce precise, unexplained counts. Only one card offers a next action. Founding date does not establish continuous founder ownership.

**Replace headlines:**
- “Give every rep a shortlist for their territory.”
- “Size a market before you hire.”
- “Find companies that fit your acquisition thesis.”

**Replace acquisition body:**
“Check ownership and founding details against public sources. Willingness to sell stays unknown.”

**Layout:** Use equally sized, legible example outputs. Remove the unsupported “Founder-run 20+ years” inference. Give all cards the same “See how it works →” link.

**4. How it works introduction — C 8/10 · V 8/10**

Clear setup, but another oversized heading and generous spacing delay the demonstration.

**Replace:**
“From a description to a ranked shortlist.”
“Find matching businesses, check the evidence and decide who to approach.”
“Illustrative example: selling fleet GPS to California snow contractors.”

**Layout:** Reduce vertical padding to approximately 64px desktop/40px mobile. Add “1 Find · 2 Research · 3 Rank” as compact section links.

**5. Search agent — C 6/10 · V 8 desktop, 3 mobile**

“Every match” implies exhaustive coverage. The intermediate zero looks like a result. Mobile scales useful text into illegibility; the source uses a half-size desktop stage.

**Replace body:**
“Describe your market in plain language. Find matching businesses across the US, then narrow the results to your territory.”

**Bar examples:** “I found 41,300 potential matches nationwide.” / “I narrowed the example to 412 in California.”

**Layout:** Keep “Illustrative example” visible throughout. Replace pending zeroes with “Counting…” or reveal completed counts only. Replace TAM/SAM with “Nationwide matches” and “California matches.” On mobile, use a native-width map and stacked, readable counts; remove prolonged pinning.

**6. Research agent — C 8/10 · V 8/10**

The sourced-answer story is strong. “Signals” is unexplained, decorative lines substitute for evidence, and the source connectors appear ambiguous.

**Replace body:**
“Check each company against your criteria using its website and public records. See the source behind each answer, identify owners where supported, and spot what’s still unknown.”

**Bar:** “I found evidence of commercial snow removal. Here’s the source.”

**Layout:** Show short, readable source excerpts from the start. Make each connector’s destination unambiguous. Retain missing-data states beside the evidence drawer. On mobile, stack source → finding → owner vertically.

**7. Scoring agent — C 8/10 · V 7/10**

The final shortlist delivers. Floating criteria and hundreds of dots delay it; “94” has no defined meaning.

**Replace headline:** “Put your strongest matches first.”

**Replace body:**
“Choose what matters most. Scorecard ranks companies against those criteria and shows the evidence behind each fit.”

**Bar:** “I ranked Sierra Line Grounds first because it meets your highest-priority criteria.”

**Layout:** Use three aligned priority rows, then transition directly into results. Label scores “Fit /100,” with an explanation of the calculation. Keep the top company’s reasons visible.

**8. Proof — C 6/10 · V 8/10**

“Check our work” promises verifiable evidence, but the illustrated company and placeholder links cannot provide it.

**Until real evidence exists, replace heading:** “See what a researched account includes.”

**Replace introduction:**
“Illustrative example: business details, supporting sources and clearly marked gaps.”

**Layout:** Preserve the comparison. Make the sample-report action prominent. To reach 9–10, replace the illustration with a real, permission-safe report containing working source links and dates.

**9. More you can do — C 8/10 · V 7/10**

Useful capabilities, generic heading, excessive space for three short paragraphs.

**Replace heading:** “Use your shortlist in your existing workflow.”

**Use titles:** “Research your existing accounts.” / “Ask your own questions.” / “Export to CSV.”

**Layout:** Compress into a shallow three-column strip; stack on mobile. Add “Monthly plan” beside custom questions if confirmed. Keep CRM wording explicitly about CSV.

**10. Pricing — C 6/10 · V 7 desktop, 2 mobile**

The mobile table hides the offer and misplaces the highlighted column. Credits lack a usable value explanation. “Owner contacts” blurs names versus phone/email.

**Fix:** Replace mobile horizontal scrolling with stacked plan cards. Put price, allowance, inclusions and CTA together. Label premium enrichment “Phone and email enrichment, where available.”

Add a verified worked example: “Adding [N] businesses uses [X] credits; [action] uses [Y].” Publish actual values only. Explain trial expiry and charging behavior explicitly.

**11. FAQ — C 8/10 · V 8/10**

Readable and candid, but misses purchase-critical questions.

**Add:** “What uses a credit?” / “Will I be charged after the trial?” / “What happens to my saved lists?”

Use confirmed answers. Replace “premium reads” with “paid enrichment.” Keep desktop answers open; use accessible mobile accordions if space requires them.

**12. Signup and footer — C 8/10 · V 8 desktop**

Strong closing line. The form currently prevents submission without continuing; footer links mostly lead nowhere.

**Fix:** Keep “Build your next shortlist.” Add a visible “Work email” label. Retain “Next, describe the businesses you want” only if accurate. Provide submission progress, errors and a genuine next step. Remove unavailable footer destinations.

**Top 10 fixes, ranked by expected impact**

1. Make signup and critical links functional.
2. Replace mobile pricing with stacked cards.
3. Rebuild mobile demonstrations at readable sizes.
4. Replace illustrative proof with a verifiable sample.
5. Remove exhaustive coverage and guaranteed-owner claims.
6. Explain credits through a concrete worked example.
7. Shorten pinned sequences; expose outcomes sooner.
8. Show the result card in the mobile hero.
9. Explain fit scores and their supporting criteria.
10. Clarify trial expiry, charges and premium entitlements.
