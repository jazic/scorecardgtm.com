# Panel: home-v22 proof, example, copy

## A. Proof section

**Positioning strategist.** "Every" and "None" are claims, and absolutes from a pre-launch company invite someone to find the exception. Real proof is an artifact the visitor can open. *Proposal:* one public sample report on a real niche, linked from the section and from the hero.

**Skeptical CRO.** ZoomInfo also says 33M+. Show me one row where my vendor is wrong and you're right, and how often you come back empty. *Proposal:* a side-by-side of a list row and a Scorecard row, plus fill rates ("owner found for 7 of 10").

**Founder-CEO.** I want to know it works on *my* market before I enter a card. *Proposal:* type a description and get a free count plus three sample matches with no signup ("Size a market free" already points this way). This is the strongest proof, but it depends on the product.

**Conversion copywriter.** The headline is a feature label. Saying what you don't know earns more trust than "None without evidence". *Proposal:* headline "Check our work." with one evidence card that looks clickable and an honest "what we can't tell you" list.

**Data-trust expert.** Buyers judge data before purchase on three things: **provenance** (where it came from), **recency** (when it was checked) and **error rate** (how often it's wrong, measured by someone). The page covers the first two and leaves out the third. A defensible hand audit ("100 rows checked Nov 10: 91 right, 6 partly, 3 wrong") beats any big number. Coverage varies by state; say so. *Proposal:* an audit strip plus a "How we count" page with the steps.

### Converged design 1: "Open a real report" (artifact-led)

- **Headline:** Check our work before you pay for it.
- **Sub:** Here's a real report on a real market. Open any company and see the line each answer came from.
- **Structure:** one large evidence card (real business, quoted line, source URL, date checked) → a "Found / Not found" strip for that report → "What we can't tell you" → CTAs.
- **Strip:** Owner named: **[x] of [y]** · Revenue: **not found for [z]** · Sources checked: **[date]**
- **Can't tell you:** Whether they want to buy. Revenue for most private companies. A phone or email for every owner.
- **CTAs:** Open the sample report · How we count · Try it on your market
- **Real vs placeholder:** The report must be a real run on a real public niche; strip counts are placeholders until then. Show owner names only where the business publishes them itself, otherwise blur. 33.2M and the source list must match what the product reads.

### Converged design 2: "A list vs Scorecard" (comparison plus audit)

- **Headline:** Same company. Two very different rows.
- **Left, "From a list":** Sierra Line Grounds · NAICS 561730 Landscaping · Truckee, CA · 1–10 employees
- **Right, "From Scorecard":** Offers commercial snow removal, Nov–Mar ("…lots and walkways…", services page, checked Nov 4) · Owner: Dana Kessler (About page, state filing) · Revenue: not found
- **Audit line below:** We checked [100] rows by hand on [date]. [91] right, [6] partly right, [3] wrong. Here's how.
- **Source chips stay, with one change:** label where coverage varies ("State licenses · varies by state").
- **Real vs placeholder:** The audit numbers must come from a real audit, or the line comes out. The left row has to reflect what list vendors really return. Don't name a competitor.

## B. Example choice

**Consensus: keep the example, add who the user is.** "Landscapers that also plow snow" makes the main point well: you describe what a business *does*, and no industry code captures that. But the page never says who is buying, so a landscaper card in the hero reads as software for landscapers. Fix it with one line above the Search demo: **"Example: you sell fleet GPS to snow contractors in California."** It also makes "412 accounts → 4 reps" read as a sales job.

Alternatives if the founder wants to move closer to the campaign ICP (founder-led vertical SaaS):

1. **"HVAC contractors with 5–20 techs that do commercial service" (you sell dispatch software).** Core narrow-SMB SaaS; contractor licenses give strong owner evidence. *Trade-off:* it reads as generic, and the "describe, not categorize" point is weaker because HVAC is close to its NAICS code.
2. **"Independent dental practices that place implants, owner-dentist" (you sell practice-management or lab services).** Dental boards make owners easy to confirm. *Trade-off:* crowded vendor space, buyers expect healthcare data vendors, and it pulls the page toward one vertical.

Strategist: test alternative 1 in organic content, not on the homepage.

## C. Copy

**"Three jobs Scorecard does well."** It's self-praise without proof.
- Option 1: **Who it's for.** / Cards: *Give every rep a full territory.* · *Know a segment is real before you hire for it.* · *Find owners who fit your thesis.*
- Option 2: **What people use it for.** / Cards: *Fill a territory.* · *Test a new segment.* · *Build an acquisition shortlist.*

**Missing "what it does" line** (under the hero sub, or as the How it works lead):
- Option 1: You describe the businesses you sell to. Scorecard finds them, checks each one against your description, names the owner and ranks the list. You make the calls.
- Option 2: Type a sentence about your ideal customer. Get back a ranked list of real companies, with the owner and the reasons for each.

**How it works intro** (currently describes the agents, not what you get):
- Option 1: You end up with a ranked list of companies that fit, the facts behind each one, and who to call first. Here's how it's built.
- Option 2: Start with one sentence about who you sell to. Finish with a list you can work through this week.

**"Put your shortlist to work."** (meant as "and it does more"):
- Option 1: **More you can do with it.** / *Take the list into your CRM.* (CSV works with any CRM or spreadsheet.) · *Rescore accounts you already have.* · *Show your board the size of the market.*
- Option 2: **Beyond new prospects.** / *Export to CSV.* · *Score your existing accounts.* · *Size a market for a plan or board deck.*
- Avoid "CRM-ready": it implies an integration.

## Recommended package

1. **Proof:** Ship **Design 1** ("Check our work before you pay for it.") built on a real sample report, and keep **Design 2's** list-vs-Scorecard row as the first thing inside that report. Add the audit line only after a real hand audit has been run. Drop "Every" and "None" as stats.
2. **Example:** Keep snow, and add "Example: you sell fleet GPS to snow contractors in California." above the Search demo.
3. **Copy:** "Who it's for." with C-Option-1 cards. Add what-it-does Option 1 under the hero. Use How it works intro Option 1. Use "More you can do with it." with Option-1 tiles.
4. **Before publishing, verify:** 33.2M index size, the source list, owner fill rates, audit numbers and the sample report's privacy handling.
