# Scorecard: visual review and alternatives

Based on `agents-v16.html` and the contact sheet. Cards show concept end frames in reading order.

Keep each business dot identifiable across sections. Distinguish preliminary search relevance from weighted final fit; all 412 candidates remain eligible for research. These are separate directions, not twelve metaphors to combine.

## 1. Search — find the relevant businesses

**Works:** California makes scope tangible. Grey-to-blue dots communicate selection quickly. The Sierra annotation adds a human observation without interface clutter.

**Unclear:** The example does not visually establish nationwide availability. “Checked” can imply full research already happened. Deep blue forecasts a verdict before evidence or priorities appear. Exact counters beside illustrative dot counts invite a literal reading; label the example once. The search field dominates more than necessary.

**A. Market stencil** — Your words become a stencil that reveals relevant businesses without moving them.
1. A small US silhouette introduces scope; California expands into a grey dot field.
2. Three word fragments form overlapping thin stencil outlines.
3. Dots satisfying the overlap turn light blue; other dots remain visible.
4. A lifted stencil leaves blue candidates in place, with a few deep-blue preliminary matches and a coral note: “worth a closer look.”

**B. Three sieves** — Broad coverage becomes a smaller candidate pool through successive criteria.
1. Grey business circles fill a wide field.
2. A geography sieve separates California.
3. Service criteria pass blue circles through two further horizontal screens.
4. A small blue pool rests beneath the screens; excluded circles remain parked beside each level.

**C. Index pull** — Search pulls relevant dots from an orderly printed business index.
1. Many thin paper leaves carry grey dot rows.
2. Plain-language words appear along one margin.
3. Matching dots turn blue across the leaves.
4. Those dots extend on fine blue threads into one loose group beside the index, preserving their origins.

**D. Tributaries** — Many geographic streams converge into one relevant market.
1. Grey dots occupy separate regional streams.
2. California’s stream comes forward.
3. Matching circles take a blue branch while others continue grey.
4. Blue branches meet in a quiet candidate basin; a few deep-blue circles suggest stronger initial relevance.

## 2. Signals — make the evidence visible

**Works:** Pills make soft signals concrete. The final named business gives the abstract pile a useful destination. “No evidence, no pill” is a strong rule.

**Unclear:** The histogram looks already scored. Its descending height can imply strongest means smallest. The implementation groups roughly four matches per dot, then treats one dot as one business. Sources never become visible artifacts. A “Revenue · no evidence” pill also contradicts the caption.

**A. Source orbit** — One business holds five signals, each tethered to its source.
1. A candidate dot comes forward.
2. Website, social and public-record paper fragments surround it.
3. Supported phrases become pills along connecting threads.
4. Five pills orbit the named business; every thread still ends at a source fragment.

**B. Margin harvest** — Research lifts useful phrases out of printed source material.
1. Three sparse source sheets unfold.
2. A reading rule crosses their lines.
3. Blue underlines detach into signal pills.
4. Five pills gather beside the business dot, with fine leaders returning to the original passages.

**C. Evidence weave** — Separate source strands assemble a business’s evidence bundle.
1. Three labelled source strands enter horizontally.
2. Supported observations appear as blue pill-shaped knots.
3. Strands cross and gather around one business circle.
4. A compact woven bundle retains five readable signal tags and visible source tails.

**D. Specimen fan** — A business opens into a small fan of sourced observations.
1. One dot is selected from the candidate field.
2. Five narrow paper slips unfold from it.
3. Each gains a signal and a tiny source reference.
4. The fan opens fully around Sierra Line Grounds; empty slips never appear.

## 3. Score — explain why these rise

**Works:** Importance tiers explain weighting. Segments suggest additive contributions. Reordering delivers a satisfying payoff with reasons.

**Unclear:** Pills dissolve rather than visibly becoming contributions. Bar lengths do not explain the displayed numeric scores. Grades, scores, names, reasons and a legend pull the ending toward a dashboard. Show that priorities belong to the user; avoid implying more evidence always means better fit.

**A. Weighted towers** — Evidence builds five unequal towers beneath business circles.
1. Your priorities assign three contribution sizes.
2. Each business receives only its supported pieces.
3. Pieces stack visibly.
4. Towers sort tallest first; the leading tower retains two plain-language reasons.

**B. Balance test** — Important evidence outweighs numerous minor signals.
1. Signals become large, medium and small weights.
2. Business circles receive their supported weights.
3. A balance demonstrates two important signals outweighing several minor ones.
4. Five circles form an ordered shortlist beneath the balance, their decisive weights attached.

**C. Evidence race** — Weighted contributions move businesses along parallel tracks.
1. Five circles share a starting line.
2. Priorities set the length of each signal’s step.
3. Supported steps advance each circle.
4. Unequal endpoints reveal order; labelled blue trail segments explain the leader.

**D. Fit rosettes** — Weighted evidence becomes a distinct radial signature.
1. Each business starts as a circle.
2. Your priorities set petal sizes.
3. Supported signals add their corresponding petals.
4. Five rosettes arrange by total contribution; the first exposes its two largest labelled petals.
